Hardware · UHF RFID · any bag you pack the same way twice

Glance. Then go.

A keychain fob clipped to a bag, box, bucket, case or truck bin. Pick it up and the fob checks that every tagged item is inside. Green means everything's there. Red means something's missing. No phone at the bag.

The problem

Anyone who packs the same roster of expensive items over and over finds out something is missing only after they have left.

One missing glove.

A $125 lacrosse glove left on a field or locker-room floor is gone within minutes. You find out in the driveway, not at the field. (The founders' own story — see About.)

One missing drill.

A $300 drill or multimeter left at a job site walks away within the hour. The truck pulls out with a tool bag that looks full.

One missing sample, lens or airway kit.

A jeweler's sample case, a photographer's lens roll, a medic's go-bag: the same list every time, checked by memory, until the one time memory fails.

AirTag finds the bag. This checks what's in it. Trackers follow the container; if an item is left behind, the bag still gets home and the tracker is satisfied. The only direct competitor (a pocket RFID scanner aimed at photographers) needs a phone app at scan time.

How it works

Setup takes a few minutes. After that, it's silent.

STEP 1

Tag your gear — one tag per item

Passive UHF RFID tags, no batteries. One tag per item is a locked design rule: spread tags read 100%; tags bunched together read 29–43% (build/19).

STEP 2

Clip the fob to the bag

It lives there. Wakes on motion from a deep-sleep state; no button, no app to open.

STEP 3

Pick up the bag. Glance.

The fob scans for a short window on pickup, compares against the list it stores, and holds the light until the bag stops moving. Green = go. Red = check.

Questions people ask

How is this different from AirTag?
AirTag tells you where your bag is. This tells you what's in it. Different problem, different product.
Do I need a phone to use it?
Only during setup, to say which tags belong to which bag. After that the fob runs on its own: pick up the bag, glance, walk.
Does it work when the bag is wet?
Wet gear is the hardest physics case for a fabric bag, and the one we tested first. A soaked, packed hockey bag read 12 of 12 tags on every pass in the July bench session (build/23). That is one bag in one house; a second bag replication is on the plan.
Does it work in a metal truck box or a vehicle?
Untested. Metal shadows UHF and needs on-metal tags; a steel box is a harder case than a fabric bag. It is on the candidate-segment list with that exact question attached, not a claim.
How long does the battery last?
Not measured yet. The production design targets a month between charges; the number will be published when the power measurements on the Walkthrough page exist. The current dev kit is a physics rig, not a battery-life demo.
How private is my data?
The item list lives on the fob and in the app on your phone. Nothing leaves your devices; the v1 plan is local-only with no accounts. The tag IDs are opaque — a stranger with a reader can't learn what's in your bag.

Next steps · phase language, no dates

The order of operations

This is an evenings-and-weekends project run by two founders with day jobs. Phases are sequenced by what each one unblocks, not by calendar. Every item in Phase A costs $0.

Phase AMeasure
  • Bench session 1 on the ODM samples (T0–T7; see Walkthrough). Output: module current at 20/23/26 dBm, boot time from power-on, sleep-wake behaviour, roster read through the Gate-1 bag, missing-item detection, antenna-fit check.
  • Gate 2 drain test on the v1 fob: 10 timed pickup cycles → mAh per event; 60 min idle → standby draw; optional cold-soak variant (build/25 §5).
  • Send the supplier the measured numbers and the open questions (pin table, EN spec, UART level, min supply, smallest antenna, modular FCC, NRE) — through the claim gate, nothing unsourced.
Phase BDecide
  • ODM semi-custom vs self-build. The unit cost is roughly a wash; the decision hinges on whether the ODM's certification offer is modular and inheritable, their mainboard NRE, and whether a 5 V-only module is acceptable (see Risks). Decide after Phase A data and the supplier's answers — "not this week" (build/29 §10).
  • FCC path. $0 action: itemised quotes from 2–3 named test labs / TCBs (one US, one China) for this device class — §15.247 FHSS + §15B + composite simultaneous-transmit + SAR. This resolves the single largest cost unknown.
  • Brand name + trademark check. Shortlist exists with domain availability (build/20). The name locks the App Store bundle ID permanently on first submission.
  • $65 provisional patent before any public disclosure or crowdfunding page (build/21 §5, §8). Few-hours attorney FTO triage against the two named patent families before committing real money.

  • Fob-autonomous verdict: repeat ~3 wet passes scored by the fob's own GREEN/RED, not the laptop.
  • Second bag replication (n=2) and a car-drive motion capture to set the pickup-vs-transport thresholds.
  • Apple Developer ($99/yr) + Google Play ($25) enrolment when the name is set.
Phase DFirst custom board
  • EVT board with the production power tree (ESP32-C3 deep sleep + accelerometer wake + gated UHF rail). Deep-sleep firmware is new work and can only be validated on this board, not the dev kit (build/25 §3).

  • Module samples for A/B if self-building (~$200–250, needs founder approval; build/21 §5).
Phase EPre-launch
  • Crowdfunding readiness per build/21 §7: prototype that demos on video, near-final industrial design, manufacturing + certification quotes in hand, email list built well ahead of launch, provisional filed first.
  • Channel conversations with teams / hockey organisations (the lowest-CAC lever).

Gates and stop conditions

What authorises the next spend, what forces a redesign. These are the bars already locked in the stream state; nothing here is new.

Locked bars (product, Phase 1)

  • True-GREEN ≥90% · false-GREEN <2% · true-RED ≥95% · battery ≥2 weeks · motion false-trigger <1 per hour — measured statistically, not as "0% false positives" (STATE.yaml probabilistic_validation).
  • Gate 1 (wet bag): ≥85% union read, 0 reads of a removed item. Passed on n=1 (build/23).
  • Working thresholds for calibration: ≥70% in-bag aggregate = continue; 50–70% = iterate placement; <50% = architecture insufficient (calibration_testing_v2).

Stop / redesign conditions

  • A gate fails → architecture rethink, not push-through (locked rule).
  • No commercial spend until Gate 1 and Gate 2 both close (build/21 §1).
  • No ODM NRE until the supplier gives a firm price, lead time, modular-cert answer and the 5 V / antenna questions in writing (build/29 §10).
  • No public page, pre-order or crowdfunding draft before the $65 provisional is filed (build/21 §7).
  • No capital commitment before an attorney FTO triage (build/21 §8).

Tech specs · production architecture under evaluation

Inside the fob

What the shipping device is planned to contain, which reader modules are candidates, and the things nobody has measured yet — called out, not hidden.

Architecture

Deep-sleep design from the power plan. Only the always-on rail is live between scans; the UHF reader's rail is switched off entirely.

Diagram follows build/25 §3. One known correction from the sample review: the rail is drawn at 4.0 V, but the candidate ODM module is specified at 5 V with no stated lower limit, so that rail must become a 5 V boost unless the supplier qualifies a lower supply in writing (build/29 §3.7).

The scan event

What happens in the ~30 seconds after the bag is picked up.

0 ms

Motion interrupt

Accelerometer any-motion wakes the MCU from deep sleep. A pickup-signature check keeps car rides from starting scans (v1.1 motion logic; thresholds to be set from a drive capture).

boot

Reader rail up

MCU raises EN; reader boots and answers on UART. Boot time from power-on is UNKNOWN for the ODM module — bench test T4 measures it.

scan window

Multi-pass inventory

Firmware runs 5 polling rounds and takes the set-union of EPCs seen, compared against the stored baseline. A missing item must be absent from every round. Window length is unvalidated: in the soaked bag the last tag arrived anywhere from <1 s to 38 s (build/23), so "~30 s" is a design target, not a measured spec.

verdict

Light holds until still

GREEN or RED on the light pipe, held until the bag stops moving (2 s debounce). Then EN low, rail off, back to deep sleep.

Testing walkthrough · bench session 1 for non-engineers

What we test, why it matters, what "pass" looks like

Each card is one test from the plan. The result slot stays empty until the session runs; the last block shows the results the v1 prototype has already produced.

Session shape. About 2.5 hours at the bench with both ODM modules, a bench power supply or current meter, and the 12 tagged gear items from the July test. No soldering for the core tests. Everything is logged to CSV with the settings in the header so a result can be re-run and checked.
T0

Check the wires before applying power

Plain English
With everything unpowered, confirm which wire is power, ground, transmit and receive with a multimeter, click the antenna on, then power the module alone and measure the voltage on its transmit pin.
Why it matters
The harness is four identical red wires and the pin table isn't published. One wrong wire destroys a free sample. The transmit-pin voltage decides whether the production microcontroller can talk to this module directly or needs a level shifter — a real cost and board-space question.
Pass bar
Four continuities map cleanly; transmit idles at ≤3.3 V (good) or ~5 V (needs a shifter).
Result
T1

Does the laptop see it, and does it answer?

Plain English
Plug in, confirm the USB adapter enumerates, then let the bench tool try each likely serial speed until the module replies with its version string.
Why it matters
Proves the module is alive and tells us which protocol and speed it shipped with (the datasheet default is 9600; our v1 firmware uses 115200). The version string goes verbatim into the supplier report.
Pass bar
Version string for both modules; the speed that answered; region and power settings read back.
Result
T2

Read all 12 tags laid out on the table

Plain English
Tags spread 20–30 cm from the antenna, 30-second scan, three times, at full power. Count how many of the 12 known tags appear and whether any unknown tag IDs show up.
Why it matters
The easy case. If a module can't read 12 tags on a table it has no business in a bag. "Phantom" tags would mean corrupted reads — a false GREEN risk.
Pass bar
12/12 in every run; time-to-all-tags recorded; zero phantom IDs. (JT-8270 runs at its 25 dBm max.)
Result
T2b

Read the packed hockey bag the way the fob would

Plain English
Same bag layout that passed in July. Run ten short bursts of five polling rounds each — exactly the window the fob uses on a pickup — and count how many bursts find all 12.
Why it matters
This is the GREEN-light promise under real conditions, measured per pickup instead of as a long cumulative scan. It's also the first head-to-head of the ODM module against the v1 module that already passed (T2e).
Pass bar
n of 10 bursts at 12/12 (reported, not thresholded yet); median time-to-all.
Result
T2c

Take one item out. Does the fob notice?

Plain English
Remove one gear item at a time (five different items), close the bag, scan in five-round bursts. The removed item's tag must never appear; the other eleven must still read.
Why it matters
This is the RED-light promise — and the trust risk. A false "all here" when a helmet is on the floor is the product-killer. Zero false-present is the bar the whole concept rests on.
Pass bar
0 false-present across all five removals; others 11/11.
Result
T2d

Turn the power down. What do we lose?

Plain English
Best bag layout, scanned once at 23 dBm and once at 20 dBm instead of the 26 dBm maximum.
Why it matters
Lower transmit power means less battery per scan and a smaller certification exposure. The dry tests suggested coverage is driven by tag spacing, not power — if that holds, we can trade power for battery life.
Pass bar
Reads/s and coverage recorded at each level (informational).
Result
T3

How much current does it actually draw?

Plain English
Power the module from a supply that reads current. Measure it idle, while scanning at 20/23/26 dBm, and asleep. Also measure the voltage right at the module while it transmits.
Why it matters
This is the battery-life number. Every mAh figure on the Tech Specs page is a datasheet guess until this runs; the reader's transmit draw isn't published anywhere. The pad-voltage check catches a sagging supply that would make the current reading meaningless.
Pass bar
mA per state (and peak if the meter can); pad voltage ≥4.75 V during transmit.
Result
T4

From power-on to first tag: how long?

Plain English
Switch the module's power on and time how long until it answers, then until it reads its first tag. Ten trials.
Why it matters
The production fob keeps the reader fully off between scans and powers it up on pickup. Boot time comes straight out of the "glance in a few seconds" experience and the scan-window energy budget. The supplier asked for this number too.
Pass bar
Median and max milliseconds, reported as an upper bound (manual switching adds human delay; a hardware-triggered version is a later test).
Result
T5

Sleep and wake: does it forget its settings?

Plain English
Put the module to sleep with its own command, wake it, time it, and read its region and power settings back before and after.
Why it matters
The protocol manual says a sleep-wake resets the chip and may reset parameters. If so, the firmware must re-send settings on every wake — which costs time and energy on each pickup — or the fob must use the power-gating path instead. This decides the gating strategy.
Pass bar
Median / max wake time; whether region and power survived.
Result
T6

Does the antenna fit the case?

Plain English
Measure the antenna stack (40 × 40 × 5 mm) against the current case cavity (42 × 38 mm) and record the full module + antenna + cable height.
Why it matters
It already doesn't fit on paper. The answer is either a slightly bigger case or a smaller antenna from the supplier — and a smaller antenna may read worse. This feeds the case revision and the supplier questions.
Pass bar
Recorded dimensions; decision: grow cavity to ≥42 × 42 or request a smaller ceramic.
Result
T7

Can the module's own enable pin replace a power switch?

Plain English
With one extra wire to the EN pad: pull it low and measure the leftover current; raise it and time how fast the module answers.
Why it matters
If EN-low draws microamps and wakes in ~100 ms, the production board can drop an external load switch — one fewer part, simpler layout. If not, the switch stays. Optional for session 1.
Pass bar
µA-class with EN low and answers within ~100 ms.
Result

Layer 3 — certification, the fork that dominates the capital math

What is settled PROVEN

  • Full FCC Certification via a TCB is mandatory before any US sale — a 902–928 MHz frequency-hopping transmitter has no self-declaration path.
  • The pre-certified BLE module only removes re-testing the BLE radio; the device still needs its own FCC ID, host testing, and a composite simultaneous-transmit check.
  • RF-exposure (SAR) evaluation is triggered for a body-worn device at this reader power.
  • Inheriting an ODM's grant via Change-in-ID avoids a per-device retest only if they hold a valid grant on the identical configuration and sign a permission letter.

Margin, and the real constraint

Gross margin at $50

Self-build: landed $26–30 @1k → ~40–48% gross. ODM path: landed $37–44 → ~12–26% gross (arithmetic on the build/29 §10 frame; no file states this number, so it is derived). Both are before overhead, acquisition cost, returns, support, payment fees or a marketplace cut (~15% on Amazon). At $50 the ODM path does not pencil unless its certification advantage is real — the reason that decision waits for quotes. ESTIMATE

Acquisition cost is the binding constraint

This is category creation — nobody searches for "RFID bag content checker". Allowable CAC is modelled at ~$8–12 per unit. Levers that beat paid D2C: team/B2B sales (one sale = 10–15 units), equipment-maker licensing (factory-applied tags), and a crowdfunding launch that is the acquisition event. LIKELY
